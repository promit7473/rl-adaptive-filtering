\begin{abstract}
Classical adaptive filters need a step-size matched to the noise
statistics, hand-designed variable step-size rules can diverge under
distribution shift, and supervised meta-learning (Meta-AF) requires
clean target-domain references at training time.
We present a \emph{hybrid BPTT+RL} controller for leaky-NLMS, trained
entirely on synthetic noise: truncated back-propagation through a
differentiable filter is coupled with PPO, so gradient structure and
label-free reward optimisation reinforce each other.
A 2-layer LSTM policy maps an 11-D error-derived state to step-size and
leakage actions on top of a decaying base schedule; three auxiliary
heads (error, signal, and noise-class prediction) force it to model the
filter dynamics, and RL$^2$ meta-episodes enable within-episode regime
inference.
At SNR$=10$\,dB the hybrid beats NLMS on \pend{8/8} synthetic noise
families and supervised Meta-AF on \pend{4/8}, transfers zero-shot to
real MIT-BIH ECG (\pend{$+6.7$}\,dB over NLMS on 50\,Hz powerline
interference never seen in training; paired Wilcoxon
\pend{$p{<}10^{-7}$}), and records \pend{$0/200$} divergent runs.
Code: \url{https://github.com/promit7473/rl-adaptive-filtering}
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
adaptive filtering, hybrid BPTT+RL, meta-learning, zero-shot transfer,
variable step-size LMS, non-stationary noise, ECG denoising
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Adaptive filters perform online interference cancellation at $O(M)$
per-sample cost \cite{haykin2014adaptive,widrow1985adaptive,sayed2003fundamentals}. Real environments are rarely
stationary \cite{nikias1995signal}: variable step-size (VSS) methods
\cite{kwong1992variable,aboulnasr1997robust} adapt $\mu$ via
hand-designed rules tuned per noise type, and we show empirically that
VSS-LMS (Aboulnasr) can diverge catastrophically on regime-switch noise.
Supervised meta-learning of filter updates (Meta-AF
\cite{casebeer2022metaaf}) back-propagates through a differentiable
filter but requires real target-domain clean signals at every training step,
limiting applicability when real clean references are unavailable.
RL has been applied to communication hyperparameter control
\cite{calabrese2018learning,mismar2019deep} but not, until recently,
to online adaptive filtering.

We pursue a \emph{hybrid} approach: combine the structured gradient signal
of truncated BPTT (as in Meta-AF \cite{casebeer2022metaaf}) with the label-free, reward-driven
optimisation of PPO (as in RL), trained on synthetic noise only; the
resulting controller is then evaluated zero-shot on real ECG.
The key insight is that BPTT provides
strong gradient signal for learning \emph{what the filter should do}
(step-size scheduling), while RL provides the flexibility to optimise
\emph{non-differentiable objectives} (convergence speed, robustness)
using only synthetic rewards.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
(i) A hybrid BPTT+RL framework unifying Meta-AF-style \cite{casebeer2022metaaf}
gradients with PPO, augmented by three auxiliary heads that instil a world
model of signal and filter dynamics.
(ii) An 11-D feature state with a decaying base step-size schedule that
is provably weight-bounding (Prop.~1).
(iii) The hybrid controller beats NLMS on \pend{8/8} noise families and
supervised Meta-AF on \pend{4/8}, and transfers zero-shot
to MIT-BIH ECG (\pend{$+6.7$}\,dB over NLMS on $50$\,Hz powerline).
(iv) Empirical stability: \pend{$0/200$} divergent runs vs.\
\pend{$14/40$} for Aboulnasr's VSS.

\subsection{Signal and filter model}
Fig.~\ref{fig:arch} overviews the system.
A clean signal $d[n]$ is corrupted by additive noise $v[n]$.
With filter weights $\mathbf{w}_n\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{M}$ and tap vector
$\mathbf{u}_n\!=\![y[n],\ldots,y[n{-}M{+}1]]^{\top}$,
the output and error are $\hat{d}[n]\!=\!\mathbf{w}_n^{\top}\mathbf{u}_n$ and
$e[n]\!=\!d[n]-\hat{d}[n]$.
The leaky-NLMS update is:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{w}_{n+1} = \lambda\,\mathbf{w}_n
    + \frac{\mu}{\|\mathbf{u}_n\|^2 + \epsilon}\,e[n]\,\mathbf{u}_n,
  \quad \mu>0,\ \lambda\in(0,1].
  \label{eq:leaky_nlms}
\end{equation}
NLMS normalization removes the dependence of convergence rate on input power
\cite{bitmead1980convergence,nagumo1967learning}, making $\mu$ a well-scaled
action for the controller.
Filter order $M{=}16$, $\epsilon{=}10^{-8}$.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 1 (Weight boundedness).}
\emph{For $\mu_t\!\le\!\mu_{\max}$, $\lambda_t\!\le\!\lambda_{\max}{<}1$, and
$|e[n]|\!\le\!e_{\max}$ along a trajectory,
$\|\mathbf{w}_n\| \!\le\! \lambda_{\max}^n\|\mathbf{w}_0\|
+ \mu_{\max}\,e_{\max}/[\sqrt{\epsilon}(1\!-\!\lambda_{\max})]$.}
The proof follows by iterating \eqref{eq:leaky_nlms} using
$\|\mathbf{u}_n\|^2{+}\epsilon\!\ge\!\epsilon$. Our action space enforces
$\lambda_{\max}{=}0.999$, $\mu_{\max}{=}2.0$; the
$\lambda_{\max}{<}1$ contraction is the structural safeguard absent from
$\lambda\!\equiv\!1$ rules and consistent with the \pend{$14/40$} Aboulnasr
divergence rate observed empirically.

Eight families: \emph{training} $\mathcal{F}_{\rm tr}$\,=\,\{Gaussian,

regime-switch\};
\emph{held-out} $\mathcal{F}_{\rm ood}$\,=\,\{$\alpha$-stable,
Poisson-rate burst, chirp interferer\}.

\subsection{State, action, and reward}
At each episode the environment samples a task $\tau$
(signal kind, $f\!\in\!\mathcal{F}_{\rm tr}$, SNR).
The per-step state is a squashed 11-D feature vector
$\phi_t\!\in\![-1,1]^{11}$; temporal context is carried by the
controller's recurrent state rather than an explicit window:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
    \phi_t &= \tanh\!\big(\mathbf{c}\odot[e_t,e_t^2,\Delta e_t,
             \Delta^2\!e_t,\log(1{+}P_u),\\
             &\qquad \log(1{+}P_e),\rho_e,\mu_t,\lambda_t,|\text{grad}_t|,\text{sgn}(\Delta e_t)]\big),
  \end{split}
  \label{eq:state}
\end{equation}
where $P_u{=}\|\mathbf{u}_t\|^2/M$, $P_e{=}e_t^2$,
$\rho_e{=}e_te_{t-1}/(\hat{\sigma}_e^2{+}\epsilon)$ with
$\hat{\sigma}_e^2$ an exponential moving average of $e_t^2$, and
$|\text{grad}_t|{=}|\mu_t e_t|/\sqrt{\|\mathbf{u}_t\|^2{+}\epsilon}$.
The constant scaling vector is set to $\mathbf{c} = [5.0, 2.0, 5.0, 5.0, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0, 4.0, 10.0, 2.0, 1.0]^{\top}$, and the step-size and leakage features are centered as $(\mu_t - 0.5)$ and $(\lambda_t - 0.85)$, respectively.
The final four features---current $\mu_t$, leakage $\lambda_t$, gradient
magnitude, and error-change sign---give the controller direct feedback
about its own past actions, enabling it to learn step-size reduction
after convergence.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Base step-size schedule.}
Rather than outputting $\mu_t$ directly, the controller outputs an
adjustment $\Delta\mu_t$ on top of a deterministic decaying schedule:
\begin{equation}
  \mu_t = \mathrm{clip}\big(\mu_0(t) + 0.3\,\Delta\mu_t,\,
          \mu_{\min},\,\mu_{\max}\big),
  \label{eq:schedule}
\end{equation}
with $\mu_0(t) = 0.8\max\!\big(0.05,\;1 - 0.8\,t/T\big)$,
where $T$ is the episode length and $\Delta\mu_t\!\in\![\mu_{\min},\mu_{\max}]$
is decoded log-linearly from the actor output. This biases the controller toward
high-$\mu$ early (fast convergence) and low-$\mu$ late (low
misadjustment) while preserving the LSTM's ability to override the
schedule when the environment is non-stationary.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Shaped reward.}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reward}
  r_t = -\log(1 + e_t^2) + \beta\,\Delta\log\bar{e}^2
        - \alpha\,|\text{med}(\mathbf{e}_{-K})|
        - \gamma \max_K |\mathbf{e}_{-K}|
\end{equation}
where $\Delta\log\bar{e}^2$ is the log-improvement in running-average error,
the last two terms are robustness penalties over the most recent
$K{=}64$ errors, and $\alpha{=}0.05$, $\beta{=}0.15$, $\gamma{=}0.02$.
A terminal bonus $0.3\,(-\log(1{+}\mathrm{SS\,MSE}))$ and fixed penalties
for $|e_t|{>}50$ or weight-norm clipping complete the reward.

\subsection{Hybrid BPTT+RL training}

The training alternates between two phases per iteration:

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Phase 1: BPTT.}
The controller processes a batch of $B$ episodes of length $T$ through
the differentiable NLMS filter. Gradients flow from the loss through
$e_t$ $\to$ $\mathbf{w}_t$ $\to$ $(\mu_t,\lambda_t)$ $\to$ controller
parameters $\theta$ with truncation window $\tau{=}64$ steps.
The BPTT loss combines MSE with three auxiliary losses:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{BPTT}} = \underbrace{\sum_t e_t^2}_{\text{MSE}}
  + \alpha_e\,\mathcal{L}_{\hat{e}} + \alpha_d\,\mathcal{L}_{\hat{d}}
  + \alpha_f\,\mathcal{L}_{\hat{f}},
  \label{eq:bptt_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\hat{e}}{=}\text{MSE}(\hat{e}_{t+1},\,e_{t+1})$ forces
one-step-ahead error prediction,
$\mathcal{L}_{\hat{d}}{=}\text{MSE}(\hat{d}_{t+1},\,d_{t+1})$
forces clean-signal prediction (the key addition over Meta-AF \cite{casebeer2022metaaf}), and
$\mathcal{L}_{\hat{f}}{=}\text{CE}(\hat{f},\,f)$ classifies the noise
family, with weights $\alpha_e{=}0.2, \alpha_d{=}0.3, \alpha_f{=}0.1$.
Early-episode errors receive an extra convergence weighting and a
median-error robustness term mirrors the reward in \eqref{eq:reward}.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Phase 2: PPO.}
After a BPTT warmup (first $K_{\rm warmup}$ iterations), PPO
\cite{schulman2017ppo} further optimises the policy from shaped
rewards using GAE($\lambda$) \cite{schulman2016gae}. The LSTM state
persists across $K_{\rm meta}{=}3$ consecutive episodes within a
meta-episode (RL$^2$ \cite{duan2016rl2,wang2016learning}), enabling
task inference without explicit noise identification.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Training distribution.}
Training episodes randomize over six synthetic signal morphology
classes (multitone, AM, sine, synthetic ECG-like pulses, random
pulses, square bursts) mixed with the five training noise families at
SNR $\in\{0,\ldots,20\}$\,dB; none are derived from real biomedical
recordings. Real MIT-BIH ECG is used only at evaluation.

\subsection{Controller architecture}

\textbf{Hybrid controller.} A 2-layer LSTM$_{256}$ (${\sim}1.07$\,M
params) with separate actor and critic MLP heads; the same architecture
is used for the main results and all ablations. Three auxiliary heads branch
from the LSTM: (i) error prediction $\hat{e}_{t+1}$, (ii) signal prediction
$\hat{d}_{t+1}$, and (iii) noise classification $\hat{f}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{8}$.
\textbf{Baselines.}
\emph{RL-only:} RecurrentPPO \cite{raffin2021sb3} (no BPTT, no auxiliary heads).
\emph{BPTT-only:} Hybrid controller trained with BPTT only (no PPO).
All models use Adam \cite{kingma2014adam}; the controller runs real-time at
biomedical $f_s\!\le\!1$\,kHz.

Table~\ref{tab:final} shows steady-state MSE (SS MSE, last 25\% of episode)
at SNR$=10$\,dB over 8 noise families.
We evaluate all methods with 5 seeds and report mean across
all (seed, family) pairs.

The hybrid BPTT+RL controller achieves \pend{$-22.3$}\,dB mean SS MSE
across all eight families, a \pend{6.5}\,dB improvement over RL-only
and \pend{7.1}\,dB over NLMS. It outperforms Meta-AF on \pend{4/8}
families, including impulsive (\pend{$-26.9$ vs.\ $-20.7$}\,dB),
$\alpha$-stable (\pend{$-26.2$ vs.\ $-23.3$}\,dB),
regime-switch (\pend{$-14.5$ vs.\ $-11.1$}\,dB), and
time-varying SNR (\pend{$-22.3$ vs.\ $-21.8$}\,dB).
The signal prediction auxiliary head
$\hat{d}_{t+1}$ is the key differentiator: by forcing the LSTM to
model the clean signal dynamics explicitly, it learns step-size
schedules that Meta-AF \cite{casebeer2022metaaf} (which only minimises $e_t^2$) does not.

On Gaussian and stationary families (colored, burst, chirp), Meta-AF retains an
edge---enabled by its untruncated BPTT gradient that flows through the entire filter
history, yielding very low misadjustment. Our truncated BPTT ($\tau{=}64$) limits
this horizon; our decaying base schedule partially compensates but cannot fully
match Meta-AF asymptotically. Crucially, however, Meta-AF requires target-domain clean references during training, whereas our controller is trained entirely on synthetic data and generalises zero-shot.
All learned controllers generalise to the three held-out OOD families
($\alpha$-stable, burst, chirp) without retraining.
Aboulnasr's VSS rule diverges on \pend{14/40} (seed, family) runs; all
three proposed controllers (RL-only, BPTT-only, Hybrid) diverge on
\pend{0/200} runs (Fisher exact, \pend{$p{<}10^{-12}$}).
Fig.~\ref{fig:recovery} illustrates within-episode recovery after a
burst.

\subsection{Zero-shot ECG transfer}\label{sec:ecg}

To test zero-shot generalisation, we apply the trained hybrid
controller \emph{without retraining} to real ECG signals (MIT-BIH
Database \cite{moody2001mitdb}): 5 records (MLII lead, $f_s{=}360$\,Hz)
corrupted with 6 noise types at SNR$=10$\,dB, over 5 seeds.
\textbf{None of these signal or noise types appeared during training.}

The hybrid controller achieves \pend{$-32.7$}\,dB mean SS MSE on ECG.
The most striking result is on \textbf{powerline interference}:
\pend{$-27.1$}\,dB vs.\ NLMS \pend{$-20.4$}\,dB, a
\pend{$\mathbf{6.7}$}\,\textbf{dB mean improvement}
on a 50\,Hz periodic tone the policy has never encountered
(Fig.~\ref{fig:zeroshot}; paired Wilcoxon over $25$ record-seed pairs,
\pend{$p{<}10^{-7}$}).
The LSTM hidden state identifies the periodic residual error pattern and
drives $(\mu_t,\lambda_t)$ to suppress it without any prior knowledge of
50\,Hz.

\textbf{Auxiliary heads.}
Removing $\hat{d}_{t+1}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation}) costs \pend{3--4}\,dB
on impulsive and $\alpha$-stable
noise; removing $\hat{e}_{t+1}$ costs \pend{1--2}\,dB; removing both costs
\pend{5--6}\,dB. The signal prediction head is the single most important
auxiliary, confirming the hypothesis that forcing the LSTM to model the
clean signal (not just react to errors) is what enables superior
non-stationary performance.

\textbf{Base schedule.}
Without the decaying $\mu_0(t)$ schedule, the controller converges to
a fixed \pend{$\mu{\approx}1.1$} that is too high for stationary noise;
with the schedule, burst improves from \pend{$-18.4$ to $-27.0$}\,dB---a
structural prior that BPTT alone does not learn within the training
budget.

\textbf{Meta-episodes.}
Disabling RL$^2$ (resetting LSTM state between episodes) costs
\pend{2--3}\,dB on regime-switch and burst, where accumulated LSTM state
identifies the current regime.

\textbf{BPTT-only vs.\ hybrid.}
The BPTT-only baseline (\pend{$-19.1$}\,dB mean) plateaus on
non-stationary families; adding PPO improves burst by \pend{6.5}\,dB and
regime-switch by \pend{1.7}\,dB, confirming RL rewards provide signal
BPTT gradients cannot.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented meta-learned step-size and leakage controllers for
leaky-NLMS, trained \emph{entirely on synthetic noise} yet robust under
distribution shift. The hybrid BPTT+RL approach fills a niche between
pure RL (flexible but slow) and pure BPTT (fast but gradient-horizon
limited): the auxiliary heads---especially the signal-prediction head
$\hat{d}_{t+1}$---force the controller to learn a forward model of the
signal that the BPTT gradients then exploit for step-size control.
Unlike supervised Meta-AF, the controller never sees a real
target-domain clean reference, yet it beats NLMS on all \pend{eight}
noise families and Meta-AF on \pend{four}. The \pend{$0/200$}
divergence record and the structural $\lambda_{\max}{<}1$ safeguard make
it deployable without per-noise retuning at $O(M)$ real-time cost, and
the same controller transfers zero-shot to real MIT-BIH ECG
(\pend{$+6.7$}\,dB over NLMS on a $50$\,Hz powerline tone absent from
training).
The main limitation is the truncated BPTT horizon ($\tau{=}64$), which
leaves a small asymptotic gap behind Meta-AF on the most stationary
noise; adaptive truncation, multi-channel and acoustic-echo settings,
and $H_\infty$-robust \cite{hassibi2001hinfinity} action constraints are
natural next steps.
