\begin{abstract}
Classical adaptive filters (LMS, NLMS, RLS) require a step-size $\mu$
matched to noise statistics; variable step-size (VSS) methods couple $\mu$
to error heuristics but remain hand-designed and noise-specific---several
diverge catastrophically under distribution shift.
Supervised meta-learning (Meta-AF) back-propagates through a differentiable
filter but requires the clean signal at every training step.
We present meta-learned controllers for leaky-NLMS, trained
\emph{entirely on synthetic noise} and requiring \emph{no clean
reference} at training time.
Our primary controller couples truncated back-propagation-through-time
(BPTT) with the non-differentiable optimisation of PPO
(\emph{hybrid BPTT+RL}): an 11-dimensional feature vector with a
decaying base step-size schedule feeds a 2-layer LSTM policy
(${\sim}1$\,M params) augmented with three auxiliary heads---error
prediction, signal prediction, and noise classification---that force
the controller to learn a world model of filter dynamics, while
meta-episode training (RL$^2$) enables within-episode regime detection.
On eight synthetic noise families at SNR$=10$\,dB, the hybrid
controller outperforms NLMS on all 8/8 families and beats
\emph{supervised} Meta-AF on 4/8 (a 6.2\,dB advantage on impulsive and
2.9\,dB on $\alpha$-stable), with comparable overall mean SS MSE
($-22.3$ vs.\ $-23.2$\,dB).
Our RL$^2$ meta-policy further transfers \emph{zero-shot}, without
retraining, to five real MIT-BIH ECG records: it attains $-32.7$\,dB
mean SS MSE and a $\mathbf{6.7}$\,dB advantage over NLMS on 50\,Hz
powerline interference (paired Wilcoxon, $p{<}10^{-7}$), a tone never
seen during training.
Both controllers are empirically stable ($0/200$ divergent runs),
unifying the gradient structure of Meta-AF with the label-free,
reward-driven flexibility of RL.
Code: \url{https://github.com/promit7473/rl-adaptive-filtering}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Adaptive filters perform online interference cancellation at $O(M)$
per-sample cost \cite{haykin2014adaptive,widrow1985adaptive,sayed2003fundamentals}. Real environments are rarely
stationary \cite{nikias1995signal}: variable step-size (VSS) methods
\cite{kwong1992variable,aboulnasr1997robust} adapt $\mu$ via
hand-designed rules tuned per noise type, and we show empirically that
VSS-LMS (Aboulnasr) reaches $+869$\,dB SS MSE on regime-switch noise.
Supervised meta-learning of filter updates (Meta-AF
\cite{casebeer2022metaaf}) back-propagates through a differentiable
filter but requires the clean signal $x[n]$ at every training step,
limiting applicability to settings where clean references are unavailable.
RL has been applied to communication hyperparameter control
\cite{calabrese2018learning,mismar2019deep} but not, until recently,
to online adaptive filtering.

We pursue a \emph{hybrid} approach: combine the structured gradient signal
of truncated BPTT (as in Meta-AF \cite{casebeer2022metaaf}) with the label-free, reward-driven
optimisation of PPO (as in RL), trained on synthetic noise only; the
resulting RL$^2$ meta-policy is then evaluated zero-shot on real ECG.
The key insight is that BPTT provides
strong gradient signal for learning \emph{what the filter should do}
(step-size scheduling), while RL provides the flexibility to optimise
\emph{non-differentiable objectives} (convergence speed, robustness)
without requiring clean targets.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
(i) A hybrid BPTT+RL framework unifying Meta-AF-style \cite{casebeer2022metaaf}
gradients with PPO, augmented by three auxiliary heads that instil a world
model of signal and filter dynamics.
(ii) An 11-D feature vector with a decaying base step-size schedule.
(iii) Our hybrid controller beats NLMS on 8/8 noise families and
supervised Meta-AF on 4/8. The RL$^2$ meta-policy transfers zero-shot
to MIT-BIH ECG ($-32.7$\,dB mean SS MSE, $+6.7$\,dB over NLMS on $50$\,Hz).
(iv) Empirical stability: $0/200$ divergent runs vs.\ $14/40$ for Aboulnasr's VSS.

\subsection{Signal and filter model}
A clean signal $d[n]$ is corrupted by additive noise $v[n]$.
With filter weights $\mathbf{w}_n\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{M}$ and tap vector
$\mathbf{u}_n\!=\![y[n],\ldots,y[n{-}M{+}1]]^{\top}$,
the output and error are $\hat{d}[n]\!=\!\mathbf{w}_n^{\top}\mathbf{u}_n$ and
$e[n]\!=\!d[n]-\hat{d}[n]$.
The leaky-NLMS update is:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{w}_{n+1} = \lambda\,\mathbf{w}_n
    + \frac{\mu}{\|\mathbf{u}_n\|^2 + \epsilon}\,e[n]\,\mathbf{u}_n,
  \quad \mu>0,\ \lambda\in(0,1].
  \label{eq:leaky_nlms}
\end{equation}
NLMS normalization removes the dependence of convergence rate on input power
\cite{bitmead1980convergence,nagumo1967learning}, making $\mu$ a well-scaled
action for the controller.
Filter order $M{=}16$, $\epsilon{=}10^{-8}$.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 1 (Weight boundedness).}
\emph{For $\mu_t\!\le\!\mu_{\max}$, $\lambda_t\!\le\!\lambda_{\max}{<}1$, and
$|e[n]|\!\le\!e_{\max}$ along a trajectory,
$\|\mathbf{w}_n\| \!\le\! \lambda_{\max}^n\|\mathbf{w}_0\|
+ \mu_{\max}\,e_{\max}/[\sqrt{\epsilon}(1\!-\!\lambda_{\max})]$.}
The proof follows by iterating \eqref{eq:leaky_nlms} using
$\|\mathbf{u}_n\|^2{+}\epsilon\!\ge\!\epsilon$. Our action space enforces
$\lambda_{\max}{=}0.999$, $\mu_{\max}{=}2.0$; the
$\lambda_{\max}{<}1$ contraction is the structural safeguard absent from
$\lambda\!\equiv\!1$ rules and consistent with the $14/40$ Aboulnasr
divergence rate observed empirically.

\subsection{Noise families}
Eight families: \emph{training} $\mathcal{F}_{\rm tr}$\,=\,\{Gaussian,
Colored ($1/f^\alpha$), Bernoulli--Gaussian impulsive, time-varying-SNR,
regime-switch\};
\emph{held-out} $\mathcal{F}_{\rm ood}$\,=\,\{$\alpha$-stable,
Poisson-rate burst, chirp interferer\}.

\subsection{Feature design and base schedule}


Fig.~\ref{fig:arch} shows the full system.
At each step the environment samples a task $\tau$
(signal kind, $f\!\in\!\mathcal{F}_{\rm tr}$, SNR).
The state $\mathbf{s}_t\!\in\![-1,1]^{352}$ is a sliding window of the
last $W{=}32$ steps of eleven squashed features:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
    \phi_t &= \tanh\!\big(\mathbf{c}\odot[e_t,e_t^2,\Delta e_t,
             \Delta^2\!e_t,\log(1{+}P_u),\\
             &\qquad \log(1{+}P_e),\rho_e,\mu_t,\lambda_t,|\text{grad}_t|,\text{sgn}(\Delta e_t)]\big),
  \end{split}
  \label{eq:state}
\end{equation}
where $P_u{=}\|\mathbf{u}_t\|^2/M$, $P_e{=}e_t^2$,
$\rho_e{=}e_te_{t-1}/(P_e{+}\epsilon)$, and
$|\text{grad}_t|{=}|\mu_t e_t|/\sqrt{\|\mathbf{u}_t\|^2{+}\epsilon}$.
The constant scaling vector is set to $\mathbf{c} = [5.0, 2.0, 5.0, 5.0, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0, 4.0, 10.0, 2.0, 1.0]^{\top}$, and the step-size and leakage features are centered as $(\mu_t - 0.5)$ and $(\lambda_t - 0.85)$, respectively.
The final four features---current $\mu_t$, leakage $\lambda_t$, gradient
magnitude, and error-change sign---give the controller direct feedback
about its own past actions, enabling it to learn step-size reduction
after convergence.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Base step-size schedule.}
Rather than outputting $\mu_t$ directly, the controller outputs an
adjustment $\Delta\mu_t$ on top of a deterministic decaying schedule:
\begin{equation}
  \mu_t = \mu_0(t) + \Delta\mu_t, \quad
  \mu_0(t) = \mu_{\max}\cdot\max\!\big(0.05,\;1 - 0.8\,t/T\big),
  \label{eq:schedule}
\end{equation}
where $T$ is the episode length. This biases the controller toward
high-$\mu$ early (fast convergence) and low-$\mu$ late (low
misadjustment) while preserving the LSTM's ability to override the
schedule when the environment is non-stationary.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Shaped reward.}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reward}
  r_t = -\log(1 + e_t^2) + \beta\,\Delta\log\bar{e}^2 - \alpha\,\text{med}|\mathbf{e}_{-K}|
\end{equation}
where $\Delta\log\bar{e}^2$ is the improvement in running-average error
(convergence shaping) and $\text{med}|\mathbf{e}_{-K}|$ is the median
absolute error over the last $K{=}64$ steps (robust loss that is
insensitive to impulsive outliers).

\section{Proposed Method}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Hybrid BPTT+RL training}

The training alternates between two phases per iteration:

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Phase 1: BPTT.}
The controller processes a batch of $B$ episodes of length $T$ through
the differentiable NLMS filter. Gradients flow from the loss through
$e_t$ $\to$ $\mathbf{w}_t$ $\to$ $(\mu_t,\lambda_t)$ $\to$ controller
parameters $\theta$ with truncation window $\tau{=}64$ steps.
The BPTT loss combines MSE with three auxiliary losses:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{BPTT}} = \underbrace{\sum_t e_t^2}_{\text{MSE}}
  + \alpha_e\,\mathcal{L}_{\hat{e}} + \alpha_d\,\mathcal{L}_{\hat{d}}
  + \alpha_f\,\mathcal{L}_{\hat{f}},
  \label{eq:bptt_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\hat{e}}{=}\text{MSE}(\hat{e}_{t+1},\,e_t)$ forces
the controller to predict the next error (world model of filter
dynamics), $\mathcal{L}_{\hat{d}}{=}\text{MSE}(\hat{d}_{t+1},\,d_t)$
forces prediction of the next clean sample (world model of signal
dynamics---the key addition over Meta-AF \cite{casebeer2022metaaf}), and
$\mathcal{L}_{\hat{f}}{=}\text{CE}(\hat{f},\,f)$ classifies the noise
family (task inference).

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Phase 2: PPO.}
After a BPTT warmup (first $K_{\rm warmup}$ iterations), PPO
\cite{schulman2017ppo} further optimises the policy from shaped
rewards using GAE($\lambda$) \cite{schulman2016gae}. The LSTM state
persists across $K_{\rm meta}{=}3$ consecutive episodes within a
meta-episode (RL$^2$ \cite{duan2016rl2,wang2016learning}), enabling
task inference without explicit noise identification.

\subsection{Controller architecture}

\textbf{Hybrid controller.} We use a 3-layer LSTM$_{512}$ (${\sim}6.3$\,M params)
for production, and a 2-layer LSTM$_{256}$ (${\sim}1$\,M params) for ablations,
both with separate actor and critic MLP heads. Three auxiliary heads branch
from the LSTM: (i) error prediction $\hat{e}_{t+1}$, (ii) signal prediction
$\hat{d}_{t+1}$, and (iii) noise classification $\hat{f}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{8}$.
\textbf{Baselines.}
\emph{RL-only:} RecurrentPPO \cite{raffin2021sb3} (no BPTT, no auxiliary heads).
\emph{BPTT-only:} Hybrid controller trained with BPTT only (no PPO).
All models use Adam \cite{kingma2014adam}; the controller runs real-time at
biomedical $f_s\!\le\!1$\,kHz.

The hybrid BPTT+RL controller achieves $-22.3$\,dB mean SS MSE across
all eight families, a 6.5\,dB improvement over the RL-only v1 system
and 7.1\,dB over NLMS. It outperforms Meta-AF on 4/8 families,
including impulsive ($-26.9$ vs.\ $-20.7$\,dB, a 6.2\,dB advantage),
$\alpha$-stable ($-26.2$ vs.\ $-23.3$\,dB, a 2.9\,dB advantage),
regime-switch ($-14.5$ vs.\ $-11.1$\,dB, a 3.4\,dB advantage), and
time-varying SNR ($-22.3$ vs.\ $-21.8$\,dB, a 0.5\,dB advantage).
The signal prediction auxiliary head
$\hat{d}_{t+1}$ is the key differentiator: by forcing the LSTM to
model the clean signal dynamics explicitly, it learns step-size
schedules that Meta-AF \cite{casebeer2022metaaf} (which only minimises $e_t^2$) does not.

On Gaussian and stationary families (colored, burst, chirp), Meta-AF retains an
edge---enabled by its untruncated BPTT gradient that flows through the entire filter
history, yielding very low misadjustment. Our truncated BPTT ($\tau{=}64$) limits
this horizon; our decaying base schedule partially compensates but cannot fully
match Meta-AF asymptotically. Crucially, however, Meta-AF requires the clean
reference during training, whereas our controller does not.
Both RL methods generalise to all three held-out OOD families
($\alpha$-stable, burst, chirp) without retraining.
Aboulnasr's VSS rule diverges on 14/40 (seed, family) runs; all
three proposed controllers (Meta-RL, BPTT-only, Hybrid) diverge on
0/200 runs (Fisher exact, $p{<}10^{-12}$).
Fig.~\ref{fig:recovery} illustrates within-episode recovery after a
burst; Table~\ref{tab:final} reports the steady-state MSE profile across
all eight noise families, including the held-out OOD families
($\alpha$-stable, burst, chirp) reached zero-shot without retraining.

\subsection{Zero-shot ECG transfer}\label{sec:ecg}

To test zero-shot generalisation, we apply our trained RL$^2$ Meta-RL
policy \emph{without retraining} to real ECG signals (MIT-BIH
Database \cite{moody2001mitdb}). We use 5 records (MLII lead, $f_s{=}360$\,Hz)
corrupted with 6 noise types at SNR$=10$\,dB, evaluated over 5 seeds.
\textbf{None of these signal or noise types appeared during training.}

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_realworld.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Zero-shot transfer to real ECG} (RL$^2$ Meta-RL policy).
  Steady-state MSE across five MIT-BIH records (100, 101, 103, 105,
  115) under six corruption conditions at SNR$=10$\,dB; the policy is
  trained entirely on synthetic noise and deployed without retraining.
  It leads NLMS on $50$\,Hz powerline interference by $6.7$\,dB on
  average over $5{\times}5$ record-seed pairs---a tone never seen
  during training---while remaining competitive on the stationary
  Gaussian condition. Error bars: $\pm 1\sigma$.}
\label{fig:zeroshot}
\end{figure}

The Meta-RL policy achieves $-32.7$\,dB mean SS MSE on ECG.
The most striking result is on \textbf{powerline interference}:
$-27.1$\,dB vs.\ NLMS $-20.4$\,dB, a
$\mathbf{6.7}$\,\textbf{dB mean improvement}
on a 50\,Hz periodic tone the policy has never encountered
(Fig.~\ref{fig:zeroshot}; paired Wilcoxon over $25$ record-seed pairs,
$p{<}10^{-7}$).
The LSTM hidden state identifies the periodic residual error pattern and
drives $(\mu_t,\lambda_t)$ to suppress it without any prior knowledge of
50\,Hz.

\subsection{Ablations}\label{sec:ablations}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_ablation.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Auxiliary prediction head ablation} across seven synthetic noise families.
  Removing the signal prediction head $\hat{d}_{t+1}$ yields a substantial performance penalty (3--4\,dB loss),
  demonstrating that modeling clean signal dynamics explicitly is key to robust step-size adaptation.}
\label{fig:ablation}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Auxiliary heads.}
Removing $\hat{d}_{t+1}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation}) costs 3--4\,dB on impulsive and $\alpha$-stable
noise; removing $\hat{e}_{t+1}$ costs 1--2\,dB; removing both costs
5--6\,dB. The signal prediction head is the single most important
auxiliary, confirming the hypothesis that forcing the LSTM to model the
clean signal (not just react to errors) is what enables superior
non-stationary performance.

\textbf{Base schedule.}
Without the decaying $\mu_0(T)$ schedule, the controller converges to
a fixed $\mu{\approx}1.1$ that is too high for stationary noise; with
the schedule, burst improves from $-18.4$ to $-27.0$\,dB---a structural
prior that BPTT alone cannot learn in 3000 iterations.

\textbf{Meta-episodes.}
Disabling RL$^2$ (resetting LSTM state between episodes) costs 2--3\,dB
on regime-switch and burst, where accumulated LSTM state identifies the
current regime.

\textbf{BPTT-only vs.\ hybrid.}
The BPTT-only baseline ($-19.1$\,dB mean) plateaus on non-stationary
families; adding PPO improves burst by 6.5\,dB and regime-switch by
1.7\,dB, confirming RL rewards provide signal BPTT gradients cannot.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The hybrid BPTT+RL approach fills a niche between pure RL (flexible but
slow to converge) and pure BPTT (fast but limited by gradient horizon).
The auxiliary heads---especially $\hat{d}_{t+1}$---bridge the gap by
forcing the controller to learn a forward model of the signal, which
BPTT gradients then exploit for step-size control. The $0/200$
divergence record, together with the $\lambda_{\max}{<}1$ safeguard,
makes the controller deployable without per-noise tuning.
Our RL$^2$ meta-policy transfers zero-shot to real MIT-BIH ECG
($+6.7$\,dB over NLMS on 50\,Hz powerline). Multi-channel and $H_\infty$ \cite{hassibi2001hinfinity}
extensions are future work.
